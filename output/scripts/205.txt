I am fifty-three years old. For thirty-one years I was the head custodian at Lincoln Elementary School, which is a title that sounds small but means I ran the boilers, the HVAC filters, the floor wax for a hundred and forty classrooms, the key control system, the chemical safety logs, and every lock on every door. I can tell when a lock has been jimmied by the scratch pattern on the strike plate, and I can rebuild a sink faucet in twenty minutes. I paid off my one hundred ninety thousand dollar bungalow in 2019, working every summer shift and every weekend rental cleanup after the school board stopped overtime. I had taken an early retirement buyout that spring, because both knees were bone-on-bone and the district wanted to cut costs, so I was at home with time to recover but not enough money to make mistakes. My husband Martin was the quiet architect of our finances, the kind of man who kept a locked firebox with every deed, a label on every breaker, and a note in the back of the file cabinet that said, "If the bank asks you to sign, call a lawyer first." That was all the backstory I had time for, because Martin died of an aortic dissection eleven days before my double knee replacement was scheduled.

Tyler and Madison did not come to the hospital when Martin died. That tear was not the only evidence. The following week, Tyler and Madison spent an evening shredding papers in the hall closet office, which they had converted from a coat closet. I sorted the strips by color and paper weight, because bank statements are on heavier paper than ordinary copy paper, and the fonts are usually a specific shade of navy.
